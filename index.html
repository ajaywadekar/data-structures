Data Structures

Learn how to store, organise and retrieve data efficiently: arrays, stacks, queues, linked lists, trees, graphs, sorting, searching and hashing, all implemented in C.

Course overview

This course gives you a strong base in data structures and shows how they shape efficient algorithms. You will learn each structure's operations and applications, then practise choosing the right one for a problem.

Each week combines three lectures with one two-hour lab. Lab programs follow the lecture topic, so you write code for ideas you have just seen.

How AI is used in this course

Weekly activities use AI tools for practice quizzes, step-by-step tracing and code hints. You always check AI output yourself, and your submitted work must be your own. The full AI use policy will be posted here.

What you will be able to do

  • CO1Explain the fundamental concepts, classification and characteristics of data structures, and estimate the time and space complexity of simple algorithms.Understand
  • CO2Implement arrays, stacks, queues and linked lists in C, and apply stacks to expression conversion and evaluation.Apply
  • CO3Apply tree and graph data structures to perform insertion, deletion and traversal operations.Apply
  • CO4Implement searching and sorting algorithms and analyse their efficiency.Analyse
  • CO5Demonstrate hashing techniques, hash functions and collision-resolution methods.Apply
  • CO6Select and justify appropriate data structures to design solutions for real-world problems.Evaluate

Syllabus

Six units, 40 lecture hours. Open a unit to see its topics.

Unit 1: Introduction and Arrays5 hours

Importance and types of data structures (primitive, non-primitive, linear, non-linear); algorithms; time and space complexity; arrays, 2D arrays, matrix representation, row-major and column-major, sparse matrix, array operations.

Outcomes: CO1, CO2   Practicals: P1, P2

Unit 2: Stack and Queue9 hours

Stack concept and array representation; reversing a list, parenthesis checker, Tower of Hanoi and recursion; infix, prefix and postfix notation, conversion and evaluation; queue and its types: simple, circular, priority, Deque.

Outcomes: CO2   Practicals: P3, P4, P5

Unit 3: Linked List7 hours

Singly, singly circular, doubly and doubly circular lists; insertion and deletion; linked representation of stack and queue.

Outcomes: CO2   Practicals: P6, P7

Unit 4: Trees9 hours

Binary tree representation and applications; binary search tree and its operations; inorder, preorder and postorder traversals; AVL tree; B and B+ trees; converting a general tree to a binary tree.

Outcomes: CO3   Practicals: P8

Unit 5: Graph4 hours

Graph types (cyclic, acyclic, weighted, unweighted, directed, undirected, multigraph); adjacency matrix and list; DFS and BFS; minimum spanning trees with Prim's and Kruskal's algorithms.

Outcomes: CO3   Practicals: P9

Unit 6: Sorting, Searching and Hashing6 hours

Bubble, insertion, selection, quick and merge sort; linear and binary search; symbol table, hash functions and collision-resolution techniques.

Outcomes: CO4, CO5   Practicals: P10, P11
